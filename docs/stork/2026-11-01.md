## 1. Read, Allocate, Route: Role-Coupled Tokenization for Pixel-Space Generative Transformers

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Based on this insight, we introduce SpecSlot, a pixel-space rectified-flow Transformer that decouples output detail from the length of the global attention sequence. On ImageNet-512, it improves FID by 2.84 over the control with 3.9% fewer FLOPs, and comes within only 0.69 FID of JiT-B/16 at 38.2% lower cost.
- Authors: Jiongfan Yang, Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Zhongang Cai, Ziwei Liu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40647v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Pixel-space generative Transformers typically use one token per image patch, so capturing finer detail requires more tokens and makes global attention increasingly expensive. We observe that finer detail does not necessarily require more global communication: low-frequency structure benefits from global exchange, while high-frequency detail can be modeled locally. Based on this insight, we introduce SpecSlot, a pixel-space rectified-flow Transformer that decouples output detail from the length of the global attention sequence. SpecSlot expands each spatial anchor into multiple slots, each assigned a disjoint band of DCT coefficients; this separates anchor density from spectral subdivision. Only the lowest-frequency slot from each anchor participates in global attention, while all slots interact locally. This allows the model to increase spectral detail without enlarging the global sequence. A spatial head further adds a local correction to the spectral prediction, while the generative state, target, and loss remain in pixel space. On ImageNet-256, SpecSlot achieves a comparable final FID to a FLOPs-matched JiT control while converging faster. On ImageNet-512, it improves FID by 2.84 over the control with 3.9% fewer FLOPs, and comes within only 0.69 FID of JiT-B/16 at 38.2% lower cost. On text-to-image generation, SpecSlot surpasses JiT-B/8 on DPG-Bench by 1.06 at 40.6% lower forward cost.

## 2. Luggage re-identification: A study of strategies towards a real-world application

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: While existing approaches primarily focus on appearance-based cropped image matching under relatively clean conditions, real-world scenarios are characterized by frequent occlusions, cluttered background and crowded scenes which significantly degrade performance. We evaluate different variants on a benchmark dataset acquired in real-world environments to address the questions of selecting and merging different views and shots of the same luggage as well as training on different environment datasets for an overall ReID performance improvement in practical applications.
- Authors: Quentin Dhôte, Romaric Audigier
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40643v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Luggage re-identification (ReID) is a critical task in video surveillance for forensics, security or border management (suspicious or abandoned luggage recognition), as well as luggage management (lost luggage recovery). It enables the association of observations of the same luggage across disjoint camera views in complex environments such as airports, train stations and border crossing. While existing approaches primarily focus on appearance-based cropped image matching under relatively clean conditions, real-world scenarios are characterized by frequent occlusions, cluttered background and crowded scenes which significantly degrade performance. Yet, strategies leveraging the complementarity of multiple shots and views from different video streams is overlooked. In this paper, we study strategies for an end-to-end pipeline that operates from multiple video streams to detect and re-identify luggage. We evaluate different variants on a benchmark dataset acquired in real-world environments to address the questions of selecting and merging different views and shots of the same luggage as well as training on different environment datasets for an overall ReID performance improvement in practical applications.

## 3. Probing Spatial State Representations in Visual Foundation Models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Finally, goal-dependent candidate selection does not establish reliable continuous action utility: predicted actions respond to goal changes, yet supplying the correct goal does not reliably improve progress.
- Authors: Baiqiao Yin, Zhongang Cai, Ruisi Wang, Qiwei Liang, Lei Yang, Ziwei Liu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

An observer sees only part of the surrounding world, yet must keep track of what lies beyond the current view. Spatial perception therefore involves more than recognizing individual images: it calls for a persistent spatial state that is updated under self-motion and used to guide action. Do visual foundation models provide representations that support such a state? We study this question through four levels of spatial capability---spatial structure, object persistence, predictive spatial update, and actionability---using frozen-feature probes on ten representations under streaming egocentric observations of static indoor environments. The resulting capability profiles vary across tasks, network layers, and prefix lengths: strong readout of the visible scene does not ensure accurate object persistence or future-feature retrieval. Matched controls separate readout accuracy from reliance on visual history and motion. Native future prediction retains recoverable spatial structure, but a spatial reader trained on real features does not transfer to predicted features unchanged. Finally, goal-dependent candidate selection does not establish reliable continuous action utility: predicted actions respond to goal changes, yet supplying the correct goal does not reliably improve progress. Across the four levels, object persistence and actionability build on camera-pose readout, whereas feature prediction builds on visible-scene readout. Together, these findings motivate evaluating spatial representations not only by what can be decoded from them, but by whether that information is maintained beyond observation, updated predictively, and used for goal-directed action.

## 4. PRISMS GHZ8: Evidence for a Young Burst and Potential Merger Activity at z=10.23

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: UV-bright galaxies in the early Universe provide important constraints on the rapid build-up of stellar mass and the sources responsible for cosmic reionization. However, the physical processes that drive their high UV luminosities remain unclear.
- Authors: Ruqiu Lin, Jorge Zavala, Javier Álvarez-Márquez, Luis Colina, Marco Castellano, Adriano Fontana, Paola Santini, Lorenzo Napolitano
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40633v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

UV-bright galaxies in the early Universe provide important constraints on the rapid build-up of stellar mass and the sources responsible for cosmic reionization. However, the physical processes that drive their high UV luminosities remain unclear. Here we report JWST/MIRI LRS spectroscopy of GHZ8, a UV-bright galaxy at z=10.23 with a magnification $μ=1.23$. The spectrum shows a $\sim 5σ$ detection for the $\rm Hβ$-[OIII] complex and $\sim 4σ$ for $\rm Hα$ line, with fluxes of $3.2\pm0.9\times10^{-18}$ and $\rm 1.7\pm0.4\times10^{-18}\,erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$, respectively. Based on JWST/NIRCam imaging, we find that GHZ8 consists of two components separated only by 0.16" ($\approx600$ pc): a bright, extended main component and a faint, compact southeastern clump, hereafter referred to as GHZ8-main and GHZ8-SE. Through morphological decomposition, we find demagnified $M_{UV}=-19.91\pm0.10$ and $-18.03\pm0.15$ mag for GHZ8-main and GHZ8-SE, with blue UV slopes of $β_{UV}=-2.53\pm0.22$ and $-3.45\pm0.49$, respectively, indicating that both components host young stellar populations. In particular, the extremely blue UV continuum of GHZ8-SE requires an extremely young, ISM-poor stellar population with a high ionizing photon escape fraction, $f_{esc}= 0.6-1.0$. Combining the UV and $\rm Hα$ luminosities with the results from the spectro-photometric SED fitting, we constrain the star-formation activity of GHZ8-main, and find either a recent, short-lived burst of star formation that is now in its final phases, or continuous star formation over a relatively short period ($<50\,{\rm Myr}$). GHZ8 therefore provides observational evidence for bursty star formation in a UV-bright galaxy. Globally, GHZ8 may be undergoing a merger-induced starburst, although deeper imaging or spatially resolved kinematic information is required for a more definitive interpretation.

</details>

## 5. Difficulty Calibration Across Time for Membership Inference in Diffusion Models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: The standard approach scores an image by its reconstruction loss and flags low-loss images as members. First, across training time, we introduce Reference-Model Calibration (RMC), which compares a fine-tuned model's loss against an earlier checkpoint.
- Authors: Michael Joseph, Yedid Hoshen
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40616v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Modern diffusion models are trained on massive datasets that often include copyrighted or private images. Membership inference attacks (MIAs) address this by testing whether a given image was in a model's training set. The standard approach scores an image by its reconstruction loss and flags low-loss images as members. This often fails because some images are simply easier to reconstruct than others, so a simple unseen image can score a lower loss than a complex training image. Difficulty calibration separates memorization from how difficult an image is to begin with. We show how to calibrate along two axes of time: training time and diffusion time. First, across training time, we introduce Reference-Model Calibration (RMC), which compares a fine-tuned model's loss against an earlier checkpoint. This cleanly isolates newly memorized data and gives near-perfect auditing, even without text captions. Since earlier checkpoints are not always available, we next present Temporal Self-Calibration (TSC), which calibrates across diffusion time using only a single model. Our key observation is that an image's difficulty persists across noise levels, whereas memorization is localized in time. By comparing the loss at a target timestep against neighboring timesteps from the same model, TSC attenuates image difficulty using the model's own trajectory. Empirically, our methods beat the state of the art by a wide margin, and unlike most existing attacks, generalize across different model parameterizations such as $x$-, $v$-, and $ε$-prediction models. Code is available at https://github.com/MichaelJoseph777/TSC.

</details>

## 6. Almost Homogeneity Characterizes Almost Bi-Lipschitz Euclidean Embeddability

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: A metric space admits an almost bi-Lipschitz embedding into a Euclidean space if and only if it is almost homogeneous. We prove the sufficient direction, complementing the known necessary condition.
- Authors: Ryo Fukano
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40601v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

A metric space admits an almost bi-Lipschitz embedding into a Euclidean space if and only if it is almost homogeneous. We prove the sufficient direction, complementing the known necessary condition. More quantitatively, we bound the target dimension and the embedding constants for every logarithmic exponent $γ>1$. The bounds depend only on the covering parameters in the almost homogeneity condition. In particular every doubling metric space admits such an embedding. This answers a question of Margaris and Robinson and the almost bi-Lipschitz part of a question of Olson and Robinson. As a consequence, the difference set of the image is doubling, which answers the difference-set questions of Olson and Robinson and of Margaris.

</details>

## 7. HairDeck: Animation-Ready Stylized Hair Cards from a Single Image

- Authors: Yuze He, Yixiang Wei, Wang Zhao, Chuhao Chen, Yanning Zhou, Yong-Jin Liu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40599v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

The hair of stylized characters in games and avatars is built from hair cards: artist-placed ribbons whose geometry is the visible hair, animated by spring bones. Existing image-based methods produce realistic hair or a single fused mesh, while CHARM, the only generator of stylized hair cards, decodes a hairstyle autoregressively as a long sequence of control points. Observing that a card hairstyle is an unordered set of artist-defined primitives, we propose HairDeck, which generates it as one: each card is a single token, and a set diffusion transformer denoises all cards together. From a single image, HairDeck generates the cards and the spring-bone chains that drive them, yielding an animatable asset that exports to ready-to-use downstream formats. On CHARM's own test set, it lowers Chamfer distance by 44% and card-orientation error by more than half while running 33 times faster, and from single images it outperforms CHARM, image-to-3D pipelines and strand-based methods. Project page: https://hyzcluster.github.io/HairDeck

## 8. Beyond Ground Truth: A Heuristic Unsupervised Quality Assurance Engine for Diagnosing Calibrational Blindness in Dental Segmentation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: In real-world clinical settings, dental X-ray segmentation using deep learning methods suffers from reliability issues. To address this problem, we introduce a simple quality-checking engine called HUS-QA with three stages.
- Authors: Fatematuj Johura Mim
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40587v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

In real-world clinical settings, dental X-ray segmentation using deep learning methods suffers from reliability issues. Even when the segmentation quality is bad, deep learning models show high confidence in their predictions (calibrational blindness). Moreover, in real use, doctors do not have ground-truth masks to check their accuracy. To address this problem, we introduce a simple quality-checking engine called HUS-QA with three stages. We train it on the PRAD (Periapical Dental X-ray) dataset to build our SimCLR self-supervised ResNet-18 U-Net model, which generates the segmentation mask and then evaluates its quality. Our HUS-QA checks the segmentation mask quality without a ground-truth mask. Prediction confidence, edge sharpness, shape connectivity, and area-size penalty are the simple indicators considered in this research. Results show that standard confidence methods fail (r = -0.1394, p = 1.7864e-3, ROC AUC = 0.4275). By contrast, our HUS-QA shows a positive correlation with actual Dice scores (r = 0.4647, p = 3.7510e-28) and achieves a strong ROC-AUC of 0.8405. Therefore, HUS-QA serves as a practical tool for clinical quality control.

## 9. RADIOlogy: A Single-Pass Approach to Training-Free Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Current zero-shot anomaly detection methods often rely on complex prompt-learning techniques or computationally expensive sliding-window strategies to achieve fine-grained visual alignment to detect small-scale anomalies. While prompt-learning approaches aim to bridge this gap, they typically require a large number of ground-truth segmentation masks from auxiliary datasets, which are often unavailable in specialized domains.
- Authors: Joaquín Figueira, Giacomo D'Amicantonio, Egor Bondarau
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40580v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Current zero-shot anomaly detection methods often rely on complex prompt-learning techniques or computationally expensive sliding-window strategies to achieve fine-grained visual alignment to detect small-scale anomalies. This reliance stems from the fundamental limitation of standard CLIP architectures, where [CLS] token attention and spatial patch pooling struggle to capture subtle, localized anomaly features. While prompt-learning approaches aim to bridge this gap, they typically require a large number of ground-truth segmentation masks from auxiliary datasets, which are often unavailable in specialized domains. In this work, we show an emerging property of multi-head attention pooling in vision-language models: remarkable zero-shot anomaly localization and detection right out of the box. We translate this property into a single-pass anomaly detection technique, RADIOlogy, that does not require dense, windowed execution and significantly outperforms other training-free baselines. Instead, the method relies on a simple contrastive scoring approach that compares the average similarity between vision features and a suite of normal and abnormal prompts. Furthermore, we leverage the agglomerative features of RADIO models to enhance pixel-level anomaly segmentation using a dense anomaly refinement pipeline. Finally, we show how the vision-language family of training-free zero-shot anomaly detection techniques can be applied successfully to the medical domain with minimal modifications. Code is available at https://github.com/jacomof/RADIOlogy.

## 10. PIVOT: Progressive Intervention via Online Teacher Correction

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We distinguish two complementary mechanisms: trajectory-level intervention, which improves access to reward-bearing trajectories, and token-level supervision, which provides dense targets to guide student learning. We therefore introduce PIVOT, which inserts teacher-generated corrections into student rollouts and applies token-level KL supervision to these segments alongside the RLVR objective.
- Authors: Zhihao Yang, Boyuan Zheng, Puhui Tan, Yukun Chen, Kuan Zhang, Longze Chen, Jiaming Li, Renke Shan
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40576v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) is limited by sparse outcome-level feedback on hard reasoning tasks: when all rollouts in a group fail, rewards provide no learning signal. Stronger teachers can provide additional guidance, but this raises a central question: how can such signals be incorporated into RLVR to support successful rollouts while remaining learnable for the student? We distinguish two complementary mechanisms: trajectory-level intervention, which improves access to reward-bearing trajectories, and token-level supervision, which provides dense targets to guide student learning. Our controlled experiments show that either mechanism alone can remain insufficient, motivating their combination. We therefore introduce PIVOT, which inserts teacher-generated corrections into student rollouts and applies token-level KL supervision to these segments alongside the RLVR objective. Beyond their combination, we systematically analyze where to intervene and how to supervise, yielding a recipe for effective guidance. Across hard logic games and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, PIVOT consistently outperforms existing methods, nearly doubling mean avg@8 on logic games from 19.5% for the best-performing baseline to 35.6%.

</details>
