## 1. Posterior Matching Distillation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Distribution-based methods achieve this by matching the student's output distribution to the teacher's. We introduce Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD), a new member of the distribution-based distillation family that combines mode-covering posterior sampling with any-step generation.
- Authors: Hidir Yesiltepe, Julius Berner, Arash Vahdat, Pinar Yanardag
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Diffusion distillation accelerates image and video generation by compressing a pretrained teacher's iterative sampling process into a few network evaluations. Distribution-based methods achieve this by matching the student's output distribution to the teacher's. This matching is often driven by reverse-KL objectives, whose mode-seeking bias favors dominant teacher modes at the expense of diversity across samples. We introduce Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD), a new member of the distribution-based distillation family that combines mode-covering posterior sampling with any-step generation. At zero population loss, PMD recovers the teacher's full posterior of clean images at each trained noise level. Its stochastic student supports any-step sampling budgets within the trained timestep grid without further training. To learn these posteriors, PMD matches exponentially reweighted averages of student samples conditioned on a fixed noisy state to corresponding teacher posterior means. Under Gaussian corruption, exponentially tilted posterior mean is a single teacher evaluation at a shifted input. We prove that, matching tilted means over an open neighborhood uniquely identifies the full posterior. PMD thus supervises the entire conditional distribution through a family of mean constraints, without any auxiliary student-score network. Experiments on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B demonstrate that PMD achieves the best FID among 4-step methods on both models, while maintaining competitive or leading performance across prompt-alignment and perceptual-quality metrics.

## 2. RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training substantially improves the visual quality, text rendering, and compositional accuracy of diffusion transformers.
- Authors: Tuna Han Salih Meral, Kaan Oktay, Alec Helbling, Atakan Kara, Duen Horng Chau, Pinar Yanardag
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training substantially improves the visual quality, text rendering, and compositional accuracy of diffusion transformers. Because capabilities such as rendering requested text or composing multiple objects are inherently prompt-dependent, a natural hypothesis is that improving them requires learning complex, input-dependent transformations. To isolate how much of these gains an input-independent shift explains, we probe each RL-tuned model with a deliberately simple parameterization: a fixed set of steering vectors added to the block outputs of the frozen base model. On SD3.5-M, these vectors hold 65x fewer parameters than the RL-tuned LoRA (0.29M against 18.8M). We fit them to match the velocity predictions of public RL-tuned checkpoints, with no reward in the objective. Although the vectors are identical for every prompt, token, and sampling seed, they recover 90.0-96.3% of the reward gains of six RL-tuned image models, and 89.8% and 111.9% on two RL-tuned video models. Initialized at zero and trained with online RL, the vectors alone exceed the reward gains of weight-based RL on text rendering (OCR) and human preference (PickScore) on SD3.5-M. A constant shift thus suffices for most of the gain of policy-gradient RL post-training: without new input-dependent transformations, it steers the pretrained generator toward what it can already compute.

</details>

## 3. AnyVIO: Feed-Forward Streaming Visual-Inertial Odometry Across Platforms

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Inertial sensing offers a way to observe metric scale, but existing methods add inertial in- formation through a filter or factor graph, which reintro- duces bespoke parameter tuning and optimization costs that can grow with the trajectory.
- Authors: Omoruyi Atekha, Marcus Greiff, Ray Zhang, Luca Carlone
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26625v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Geometric foundation models (GFMs) estimate camera mo- tion and scene structure from images without requiring ex- pert parameter tuning, but either lack metric scale or rely on learned priors to infer scale, which is unobservable from monocular images alone. Inertial sensing offers a way to observe metric scale, but existing methods add inertial in- formation through a filter or factor graph, which reintro- duces bespoke parameter tuning and optimization costs that can grow with the trajectory. To bridge this gap, we present AnyVIO, a real-time, feed-forward streaming model for monocular visual-inertial odometry (VIO), which combines a streaming GFM with an inertial foundation model (IFM). Both the GFM and IFM predict the metric motion between consecutive frames, and a lightweight learned fusion head quantifies the reliability of each estimate and blends them in a single forward pass. Across nine datasets recorded from cars, legged and ground robots, and handheld and head- mounted devices, AnyVIO reduces the metric position error of its visual model by 36% while publishing each pose esti- mate 70 ms after an image is received. We further deploy AnyVIO onboard two real vehicles at up to 140 km/h, where it remains accurate in poor lighting and heavy dust.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

The class $x_{153,3}$ lies in stable degree $153$ and filtration $3$ on the algebraic starting page of the $3$-primary Adams--Novikov spectral sequence of the sphere. We show that it survives and begins a family with period $144$, answering both parts of a question of Carrick and Davies. A comparison with periodic topological modular forms and a bound on the possible differential targets prove permanence. A divisibility calculation for the mod-$3$ Moore spectrum supplies a lift to a finite spectrum with a degree-$144$ self-map. The resulting iterates have nonzero image in the height-two detector of Carrick and Davies.

We present Depth Pro 2, an approach to estimating dense metric depth for images and video at full resolution. By analyzing prior methods for monocular depth estimation, we find that they commonly trade off temporal consistency and boundary sharpness: multi-frame methods favor consistency, single-frame methods favor sharpness. We release a family of models that define a new Pareto frontier in terms of metric accuracy, sharpness, temporal consistency, and runtime. ## 6. Local orbital magnetization and Chern markers from equilibrium local currents

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Building upon recent work on stochastic estimation methods for tight-binding models, we show that all matrix elements of positive semidefinite operators defined on a lattice can be obtained at once from a single set of filtered random vectors.
- Authors: Henrique P. Veiga, Aires Ferreira, Caio H. Lewenkopf, João M. Viana Parente Lopes
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26599v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Local currents carry the orbital magnetization of a macroscopic sample and are key for modeling inhomogeneous magnetic materials and for understanding magnetoelectric and topological responses. However, producing realistic simulated images of local currents in inhomogeneous systems remains challenging. Building upon recent work on stochastic estimation methods for tight-binding models, we show that all matrix elements of positive semidefinite operators defined on a lattice can be obtained at once from a single set of filtered random vectors. This eliminates the need for independent simulations previously required for off-diagonal elements, which significantly speeds up the calculation of spatial maps of local currents. Further, we express bond currents in terms of loop currents that obey a discrete Poisson equation, with the current vorticity acting as the source term. Its solution yields a local orbital magnetization. In analogy with the global Středa relation, the derivative of this magnetization with respect to the chemical potential defines a new local Chern marker. Built entirely from bond currents, it requires no position operator and is not bound by the zero-sum rule of projector markers in open samples. Hence, it is free of boundary-compensation artifacts, and its sample average converges to the bulk Chern number in a controlled fashion.

</details>

## 7. Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost.
- Authors: Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, Yuxin Chen
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26598v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Acquiring every feature of an instance is often costly: a measurement can carry a monetary price, a time delay, or a privacy cost. Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) addresses this by learning a policy that decides, per instance, which features to acquire under a budget. Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. We construct this teacher explicitly. At training time the label and the unobserved feature values are available, and together they determine the teacher in closed form: an oracle that ranks every candidate by its effect on the downstream predictor's error. OracleImitator distills this ranking into a deployable policy by soft-target imitation. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost. Because the teacher is an explicit algorithmic object rather than an implicit optimization target, we can also measure directly how much of its ranking is recoverable by a policy restricted to what is visible at deployment.

</details>

## 8. PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates.
- Authors: Kai Lu, Chenyang Ma, Ioannis Havoutis
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates. Project page is available at: https://kl-research.github.io/pgx

## 9. Star-forming disc galaxies align tangentially with the cosmic tidal field

- Source: arXiv

Galaxy alignments are a major systematic for weak gravitational lensing and help us learn about the processes of galaxy formation.
- Authors: Calum Murray, Roman Paviot, Martin Kilbinger, Cail Daley, Ken Ganga, Michael J. Hudson
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26593v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

The alignments of galaxy shapes, with one another and with the large-scale structure of the Universe, have been a topic of considerable investigation within cosmology and extragalactic astrophysics for the last 60 years. Galaxy alignments are a major systematic for weak gravitational lensing and help us learn about the processes of galaxy formation. Red early-type galaxies are known to show strong radial alignments around other galaxies; however, blue star-forming galaxies have shown little evidence of alignment. We combine SPHEREx spectrophotometry with DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys to build a full-sky catalogue of 71 million galaxies with precise redshifts. With this catalogue we are able to measure alignment signals by splitting galaxies into colour and morphological samples. We detect tangential alignment of blue disc galaxies at $22.5σ$ and radial alignment of red spheroidal galaxies at $65σ$. By measuring the alignment signal separately around overdensities and underdensities, we show that each of these alignments has the scale and environmental dependence of a linear response to the cosmic tidal field; a response purely quadratic in the Gaussian tidal field, as in classic tidal torque theory is ruled out.

</details>

## 10. ProtoDiffusion: Token-per-Layer Generative Modeling of Multi-Layer Images

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We present ProtoDiffusion, a latent-diffusion framework built on ProtoVAE, a multi-layer variational autoencoder that encodes a design into one shared spatial prototype and one candidate vector per layer.
- Authors: Adrià Ciurana Lanau, Harleen Hanspal, Lusine Abrahamyan, Emilio Tylson Baixauli, Yu Cao, Danai Triantafyllidou, Stefano Zorzi, Gustavo Zanoni Felipe
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26586v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Graphic designs are ordered compositions of RGBA layers whose separation enables selection, movement, and reuse. Existing layered models represent each layer with a full-canvas or region-sized spatial latent grid, so representation size grows with the cumulative spatial extent of the layers. We present ProtoDiffusion, a latent-diffusion framework built on ProtoVAE, a multi-layer variational autoencoder that encodes a design into one shared spatial prototype and one candidate vector per layer. Each candidate conditions a shared decoder to reconstruct its corresponding RGBA layer. For $N$ layers, the representation contains $HW+N$ tokens before backbone-specific packing, compared with $NHW$ for full-grid-per-layer representations. ProtoDiffusion jointly denoises the prototype and a fixed set of candidate slots, conditioned on an image or text, with bounding boxes as optional layout guidance. The model leaves unneeded slots empty and determines the number of layers and, in absence of boxes, their layout, requiring neither as input. The same architecture supports both image-to-design and text-to-design. ProtoVAE achieves high-fidelity reconstruction from its compact shared latent representation, reaching 40.43 dB PSNR on layer crops. On the LayerD evaluation split, image-to-design achieves RGB L1 scores of 0.040 with bounding boxes and 0.057 without, with box-guided alpha soft IoU (0.850) comparable to the strongest fine-tuned baseline (0.871). On merged images, text-to-design reaches 66.41 FID versus 86.84 for FLUX.2 [klein], achieves a 0.860 text-accuracy score, and runs $19.1\times$ faster than the same-backbone generation-then-decomposition cascade.

</details>
