## 1. Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively.
- Authors: Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Yutong Bai, Nicolas Dufour, Patrick Pérez, David Picard
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30129v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively. They generate in the latent space of a video autoencoder, whose compression sets their cost per frame and how long each action waits to take effect. Yet world models often reuse the autoencoders of text-to-video generators, built for offline generation with no agent waiting and, without their training data and code, not retrainable at another compression. We therefore ask how, where and how much a world model should compress. We build CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders spanning multiple spatio-temporal compressions. Its members differ only in a small bottleneck on frozen features and share a diffusion decoder self-distilled to one or a few steps. We train the same world model on each autoencoder in two domains, manipulation and navigation, with two environments each. CADRE forms most of the latency-quality frontier, especially with a moving camera. Our study also leads to four findings. At strong compression, a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression. In robot manipulation, temporal compression is more effective in the autoencoder than in the world model. Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions: temporal compression costs little when the camera is still, and spatial compression when it moves. Surprisingly, with a still camera, coarser spatial tokens hurt motion far more than image quality. Code, models and a live demo are at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-21-cadre

## 2. Geometric Algebra for Neural Networks: A Survey

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Geometric algebra (GA) unifies geometric primitives and transformations in a single algebraic framework. Neural networks designed around this framework often represent features as multivectors and constrain layer operations to respect the algebraic structure, enabling principled treatment of geometric transformations and, in many cases, built-in equivariance.
- Authors: Tao Zhong, Kaison Fong, Keqin Wang, Christine Allen-Blanchette
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30121v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Geometric algebra (GA) unifies geometric primitives and transformations in a single algebraic framework. Neural networks designed around this framework often represent features as multivectors and constrain layer operations to respect the algebraic structure, enabling principled treatment of geometric transformations and, in many cases, built-in equivariance. This survey reviews geometric algebra neural networks, including their building blocks, equivariant properties, application domains, empirical performance, and software support. We provide an algebra-selection guide, an empirical comparison of GA-based and other equivariant architectures, and a failure-mode analysis covering training pathologies, deployment constraints, and performance limitations. Across the surveyed literature, the strongest evidence for GA-based architectures appears in settings with explicit geometric symmetries and heterogeneous geometric entities; evidence for broader advantages in parameter efficiency, interpretability, and dense prediction is comparatively limited and uneven. We therefore conclude that geometric algebra neural networks are not universally superior to well-tuned non-GA equivariant baselines, and outline open problems in algebra selection, numerical stability, scalable implementation, and reproducible evaluation.

## 3. ALPS: Visual State Encoders for World Models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: However, existing encoders typically specialize in only one or two of these capabilities; we therefore introduce ALPS, a pretrained visual state encoder that unifies all three in a single set of patch tokens.
- Authors: Yanqing Liu, Letian Zhang, Chengzhi Liu, Zijun Wang, Jieru Mei, Hongliang Fei, Yuyin Zhou, Cihang Xie
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30120v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

World models turn visual representations into predictions and actions. We argue that their visual foundation should combine physical-state information, native language alignment, and pixel reconstruction, so that a single representation can support prediction, instruction, and inspection. However, existing encoders typically specialize in only one or two of these capabilities; we therefore introduce ALPS, a pretrained visual state encoder that unifies all three in a single set of patch tokens. Using only still images and text, we pretrain a ViT-B/16 from scratch with language, reconstruction, and spatial correspondence objectives, and then refine it via cross-view self-distillation. In a unified evaluation across fifteen public encoders, ALPS achieves the lowest aggregate LIBERO state-probe error among ViT-B models (matching the 3.5x larger V-JEPA 2-L) while maintaining strong language alignment and accurate pixel reconstruction. Downstream, frozen ALPS tokens reach the highest mean policy success on LIBERO (85.5%) when paired with our native text tower, match or exceed leading encoders on world-model planning across PushT, Wall, and Granular, and allow predicted future tokens to be decoded directly into pixels for visual inspection. We hope this work helps establish visual pretraining as a shared foundation for understanding, prediction, and action in world models.

## 4. The role of locality and weight sharing in convolutional neural networks

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: The success of convolutional neural networks is often attributed to locality and weight sharing. However, how these two properties separately shape learning, and how their effects depend on the spatial correlations of natural images, remain poorly understood.
- Authors: Francesco Mori, Melanie Weber
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30118v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

The success of convolutional neural networks is often attributed to locality and weight sharing. However, how these two properties separately shape learning, and how their effects depend on the spatial correlations of natural images, remain poorly understood. We address these questions in a teacher-student model with a convolutional teacher acting on $P$ input positions with correlation $c$ between positions. We compare convolutional and locally connected students trained by online stochastic gradient descent, and derive ordinary differential equations that describe their dynamics exactly in the limit of large feature dimension $D$. For uncorrelated positions ($c = 0$), the two students follow the same learning trajectory, with the convolutional student learning exactly $P$ times faster. We also find that locality affects the sample complexity: a student that matches the kernel size of the teacher recovers it from $\mathcal{O}_{D}(D)$ samples, whereas $\mathcal{O}_{D}(D\log(D))$ samples are needed with a larger kernel. For $c > 0$, spatial correlations can reduce the advantage of weight sharing. The asymptotic error of the convolutional student grows with $c$, whereas that of the locally connected student is independent of $c$ and overall lower when evaluated at the same learning rate. When the learning rate is instead tuned to the training horizon, however, the locally connected student wins at early times and the convolutional student at late times, with a transition that we predict analytically.

</details>

- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Despite recent advances, current AI systems remain highly susceptible to domain shifts, particularly in biomedical imaging, where images are usually acquired under substantially different experimental conditions. Although recent domain adaptation methods have made substantial progress, significant performance gaps under domain shift persist. To measure progress in this regard, we introduce BatchBench, a leaderboard that evaluates and compares the robustness of AI systems on biomedical images from unseen experimental batches and data sources. BatchBench is built on JUMP-CP, one of the largest publicly available biomedical imaging datasets. Generated using the Cell Painting assay, JUMP-CP exhibits pronounced batch effects and domain shifts, making it a challenging testbed for evaluating robustness to distributional changes.

## 7. GoiBERT: Compact Vietnamese Language Models via Two-Stage Knowledge Distillation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Vietnamese pre-trained encoders have improved rapidly, but the strongest ones are large, multilingual-vocabulary models or depend on external word segmentation, which makes them costly to deploy.
- Authors: Quang Anh Kiet Tran, Nam Anh Tran, Manh Hung Trinh, Thanh Danh Le, Khang Nhat Hoang Vo, Huynh Dai Nhan Tran
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30067v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Vietnamese pre-trained encoders have improved rapidly, but the strongest ones are large, multilingual-vocabulary models or depend on external word segmentation, which makes them costly to deploy. We present GoiBERT, a family of two compact Vietnamese encoders with 26M and 49M parameters that operate directly on raw text. GoiBERT learns from two complementary teachers. A same-tokenizer Vietnamese encoder first teaches general-purpose representations. A much larger teacher then provides task-level guidance through soft labels that are generated without ever labeling its own training data. Across eight benchmarks covering text classification, aspect-based sentiment analysis, natural language inference, and named entity recognition, GoiBERT-large achieves the highest average score among non-teacher models. It does so with fewer than half the parameters of the strongest compact baseline and twice its throughput. GoiBERT-base retains 99% of that baseline's performance at a quarter of its size. Our analysis shows that compression is nearly lossless for sentiment and classification tasks but remains costly for inference and entity recognition, which points to clear directions for future work. Our code and models are available at: https://github.com/Nhantran65/GoiBERT.git

## 8. Facets of the X-ray polytope of doubly stochastic matrices

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We study the polytope of anti-diagonal X-rays of k x k doubly stochastic matrices, the image of the Birkhoff polytope under the map that sums the entries along each anti-diagonal. Its facets other than the coordinate facets are the minimal joint tail bounds for lattice laws of a sum of two dependent uniform random variables.
- Authors: Prashant Chaudhary, Kapil Khandelwal
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30058v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

We study the polytope of anti-diagonal X-rays of k x k doubly stochastic matrices, the image of the Birkhoff polytope under the map that sums the entries along each anti-diagonal. Its facets other than the coordinate facets are the minimal joint tail bounds for lattice laws of a sum of two dependent uniform random variables. We give an exact facet criterion in terms of the allowed edges of a tight graph. For rays of the underlying tropical fan, total support of the tight graph suffices for a facet but is not necessary, as an example with k=19 shows. Up to affine functions and positive scaling, the potentials that define a facet form a polytope whose vertices are its rays. Facets propagate by padding and by grid refinement under explicit hypotheses. We give infinite families of facets from no step potential, facets of quadratic height, superpolynomially many facets, and a classification of three-step potentials. Total support of a step potential is characterized by a Hall-type condition on block starts; with computer assistance, total support decides facets of step potentials for k<=26. The facet counts up to k=12 are found directly for k<=3 and, for k>=4, come from an enumeration by a provably complete algorithm; an independent exact recomputation reproduces its ray lists for 5<=k<=12.

</details>

## 9. What the Author Wrote, What the Agent Reads: Silent Cue Loss in Enterprise Document Ingestion

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Enterprise agents act on documents. Before an agent can follow a policy, answer a compliance question or file a ticket, some ingestion layer must turn a Word file or PDF into tokens - and that layer is where an agent deployed in the wild silently loses information.
- Authors: Yikai Zhao, Saurabh Pandey, Pradeep Kumar Misra
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30026v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Enterprise agents act on documents. Before an agent can follow a policy, answer a compliance question or file a ticket, some ingestion layer must turn a Word file or PDF into tokens - and that layer is where an agent deployed in the wild silently loses information. We show that one layer is lost by every mainstream converter - the meaning an author encodes in how text looks - and that the loss makes agents confidently wrong rather than visibly incomplete. We build a task from 76 real policy documents: recover the requirements the author singled out, scored against the file's own OOXML properties by a deterministic matcher with no LLM judge. Formatting-blind ingestion collapses from micro F1 0.690 to 0.150 (docling) and 0.075 (plain text), a gap that replicates across six answering models from two vendors - five Claude models spanning the Opus, Sonnet and Haiku tiers, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6. An agent that cannot see the cue guesses, and docling's false-positive rate on requirements the author did not flag is four times ours. A span-level leave-one-out localises the loss to two cues - highlight (+0.302) and colour (+0.262), both Holm-adjusted p < 1e-4 - while bold, italic, monospace and strikethrough each sit within +/-0.003 of zero. The same asymmetry appears in PDF. Colour is also the cue no mainstream converter preserves and no parsing benchmark scores, and it is common: 32.8% of 6,460 documents carry a deliberate colour or highlight, 10.3% on an obligation. A pre-registered test shows that typing a cue for the agent adds +0.085 beyond merely preserving it (Holm-adjusted p = 0.049). We validate the labels against rendered page images through an independent modality (kappa = 0.72) and bound what wording alone can achieve (AUC 0.664). For agent builders the practical finding is that ingestion, not reasoning, is where this reliability is won or lost, and that the fix is cheap and measurable.

</details>

## 10. ClunoGS: Compact Language 3D Gaussian Splatting via Unified Optimization

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially.
- Authors: Jian Xu, Yi Li, Ying Huang, Siyi Wu, Bingzhe Li, Yuede Ji, Miao Yin
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30013v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Language 3D Gaussian Splatting enables open-vocabulary scene understanding by associating Gaussian primitives with language features. These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially. However, pruning based only on rendering quality can remove Gaussians that have small RGB contributions but are important for semantic prediction. Once removed, their semantic information cannot be recovered by later quantization. We propose ClunoGS, a compact framework for Language 3DGS that combines semantics-aware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a unified constrained objective. Semantic supervision is used during sparsification, and the objective jointly considers rendering quality, semantic accuracy, and compactness. We optimize the unified objective with alternating primal, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates. We evaluate ClunoGS on open-vocabulary localization and segmentation using the LERF and ScanNet-10 benchmarks. Under aggressive compression, our unified, semantics-aware formulation substantially alleviates the degradation of semantic representations caused by sequential compression and semantics-agnostic Gaussian sparsification. Ablations further show that jointly coordinating sparsification and quantization better preserves semantic accuracy than applying them independently.

</details>
