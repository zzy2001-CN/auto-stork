## 1. Improving and Expanding Video Editing Capabilities Without Videos

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce Image-to-Video Transfer (I2VT), which improves pretrained video editors and expands them to new editing tasks using an image editor. Although the video editor sees only single images during training, at test time it applies the improved and expanded capabilities to whole video clips while preserving temporal consistency.
- Authors: Ji Xie, Yanhao Jia, Xiangpeng Yang, Martin Q. Ma, Sihan Xu, Erik Cambria, Louis-Philippe Morency
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34941v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Image editing models can follow a wide range of instructions, but video editing models still lag behind, limited in part by the difficulty of collecting paired source and edited videos. Is it possible to transfer advanced image editing capabilities to video editors without new paired videos? We introduce Image-to-Video Transfer (I2VT), which improves pretrained video editors and expands them to new editing tasks using an image editor. It enables efficient post-training with no architectural changes and no new videos, using only individual images. Our key idea is to let the video editor role-play as an image editor: it edits an image as a one-frame video, with the image editor guiding the video editor's predictions at each denoising step. Although the video editor sees only single images during training, at test time it applies the improved and expanded capabilities to whole video clips while preserving temporal consistency. To push the boundaries of video editing, we introduce OpenVE++, an extension of OpenVE-Bench that adds four new editing tasks useful for video production, including action changes, expression changes, body movements, and scene relighting. Our experiments across three different video editors show that I2VT improves existing editing capabilities and expands them to new tasks, and our models achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on both OpenVE-Bench and OpenVE++.

## 2. A Mesh for Every Moment

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We present MEMO (a Mesh for Every MOment), a method that reconstructs the geometry of complete dynamic scenes, captured by multiple synchronized cameras, and extracts a mesh at every frame. Existing dynamic Gaussian splatting methods either model appearance alone or target isolated objects segmented by foreground masks, and all of them recover meshes by TSDF fusion or Poisson reconstruction, whose fixed grids limit the detail they can capture.
- Authors: Roy Or-El, Jeong Joon Park, Ira Kemelmacher-Shlizerman
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34939v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

We present MEMO (a Mesh for Every MOment), a method that reconstructs the geometry of complete dynamic scenes, captured by multiple synchronized cameras, and extracts a mesh at every frame. Existing dynamic Gaussian splatting methods either model appearance alone or target isolated objects segmented by foreground masks, and all of them recover meshes by TSDF fusion or Poisson reconstruction, whose fixed grids limit the detail they can capture. At the core of MEMO is a per-Gaussian motion model inspired by Gaussian processes, which represents each Gaussian's position and rotation over time as a combination of temporal Gaussian kernels. The model keeps every Gaussian a valid surface element throughout the sequence and carries an implicit representation of the dynamic geometry, so a mesh can be derived at any time from a field defined by the Gaussians. Although common in real captures, large and fast motion poses a challenge that dynamic Gaussian splatting methods usually overlook. To account for it, we design a track-based initialization scheme that fits each Gaussian's initial trajectory to point tracks in closed form. We evaluate MEMO on Blender-Moments, a new benchmark of nine animated scenes with ground-truth meshes at every frame, and on two real-world datasets. MEMO reconstructs the most accurate geometry among state-of-the-art methods, and remains competitive in novel-view synthesis and training time. Project page: https://memo-4d.github.io

</details>

## 3. Can image generation help visual reasoning? An empirical study

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We test whether a generated helper improves the answer to a visual question.
- Authors: Xueyang Yu, Yifan Jing, Taiming Lu, Linrong Cai, Zhipeng Cai, Yunyang Xiong, Gabriel Sarch, Zhuang Liu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34935v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Language models think in words; then multimodal models might think in images. We test whether a generated helper improves the answer to a visual question. A model describes the helper it needs and then answers using the original and generated images together. Across 13 vision-language model and generator pairs, generation lowers mean accuracy by 3.7 percentage points, with declines on nine of ten tasks. Two unified models, BAGEL and Janus-Pro-7B, each lose accuracy on eight tasks. On VSTAR, adding a generated image to the same planning hint lowers accuracy by 10.47 points. Repeated image edits yield different outcomes across tasks. ChartQAPro and MathVista stay below their no-generation baselines, while ERQA gains three points after ten edits.

## 4. MERLoc: Robust Robot Localization in Dynamic Environments with Pose-Based Retrieval over 3D Gaussian Splatting References

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Robots operating in previously mapped spaces must localize accurately despite moving people and rearranged objects. Most localization pipelines match the query against a sparse set of captured reference images retrieved by appearance, which is brittle under scene change and repeating structures.
- Authors: Michael Baumgartner, Dario Mylonopoulos, Sammy Christen, Markus Gross, Moritz Bacher
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34932v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Robots operating in previously mapped spaces must localize accurately despite moving people and rearranged objects. Most localization pipelines match the query against a sparse set of captured reference images retrieved by appearance, which is brittle under scene change and repeating structures. We present MERLoc, which turns a single metric 3D Gaussian Splatting scan into a dense Functional Reference Memory. Reference views are sampled over the navigable space for the robot's morphology and sensors at a target density and rendered offline. Pre-encoding them for the chosen off-the-shelf localizer removes the reference encoder from inference. A pose-based retrieval strategy then selects references by spatial proximity and viewing direction rather than appearance, sidestepping visual ambiguities whenever a prior exists. Across one classical and four learned localizers in simulated and real dynamic scenes, pose-based retrieval matches or outperforms appearance retrieval in most cases, even when the latter is gated by the same prior. On real data, it lowers position RMSE by 15-60 % in nine of ten backend/split combinations, and the rendered memory outperforms real-image caches posed by Structure-from-Motion. The system runs onboard a Jetson Orin NX at 5 Hz, enabling a navigation policy to successfully traverse a hallway with people roaming and rearranging objects.

</details>

## 5. DynMF2: Rigid Motion Factorization by Expectation-Maximization for Monocular 4D Reconstruction and Motion Segmentation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: A single casual video leaves a moving scene under-determined: a point seen once from each place can slide toward or away from the camera and still match every frame. We constrain the reconstruction with the structure of the motion itself.
- Authors: Agelos Kratimenos, Yile Hua, Kostas Daniilidis
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34931v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

A single casual video leaves a moving scene under-determined: a point seen once from each place can slide toward or away from the camera and still match every frame. We constrain the reconstruction with the structure of the motion itself. Building on DynMF, we model a scene's motion as a handful of SE(3) trajectories, each rigid, and let every point follow one of them alone, never a blend. DynMF2 computes this factorization directly from 3D point trajectories, with no rendering, by Expectation-Maximization (EM). Its M-step fits, in closed form, every rigid motion to the points assigned to it, and its E-step reassigns every point to the motion that explains it with the least error. The background is simply one more motion, the camera's, so no masks are needed. Three tools then revise the factorization: requery adds trajectories where the rendering shows parts the tracker missed, merge joins motions that move together, and split separates those that do not. Thus the number of motions follows the scene. Without masks, DynMF2 clearly outperforms the mask-based baselines in view synthesis on iPhone and NVIDIA and in 3D tracking on iPhone. It recovers what masks leave out, from thin parts and details to small movers far in the background. Its motions segment the moving parts of MOVi-F and iTACO scenes competitively, although DynMF2 is never trained to segment. What moves in a scene is not decided in advance: it emerges from the rigid motions themselves.

</details>

## 6. A gene informed benchmark for BioScoring of pathology foundation models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce BioScore, a benchmark that quantifies how well an image encoder captures cell identities, with spatial transcriptomics of the same tissue as the reference.
- Authors: Paul Vilars, Vincent Quoc-Huy Trinh, Maria Vakalopoulou, Dimitris Samaras, Stergios Christodoulidis, Mahdi S. Hosseini
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34930v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Pathology foundation models are usually compared by their performance on downstream tasks such as classification and retrieval. While such benchmarks measure how useful a model is, they provide no insight into the biology it represents. We introduce BioScore, a benchmark that quantifies how well an image encoder captures cell identities, with spatial transcriptomics of the same tissue as the reference. A sparse autoencoder splits the encoder's patch embeddings into sparse features, which we cluster into a fixed number of groups. BioScore then measures, for each of 52 cell identities defined by marker genes, how specifically one group follows the identity's expression over the tissue. Because the autoencoder training is fully unsupervised, a match shows that the model represents the cell identity from the image alone. We score 8 pathology foundation models and 3 natural-image models. Every pathology model scores above every natural-image model (0.299--0.371 \vs 0.214--0.232), and paired tests separate three tiers among the pathology models. Unlike a supervised probe, BioScore needs no labels, builds its reference from the data and scores even from few sections, yet it ranks the encoders as a probe does. Its per-identity breakdown indicates which cell identities each encoder captures or misses.

</details>

## 7. High-Fidelity Image Matting with Pixel-Space Diffusion Transformers

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: However, existing approaches largely rely on latent diffusion models, whose compressed representations and VAE-based reconstruction introduce a mismatch with the high-frequency precision required for matting. We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting.
- Authors: Baihan Yang, Xin Lin, Yufan Wei, Baiang Li, Yuheng Liu, Juanxi Tian, Xiaohui Xie, Truong Nguyen
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34929v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for image matting by transferring generative priors learned from large-scale pretraining. However, existing approaches largely rely on latent diffusion models, whose compressed representations and VAE-based reconstruction introduce a mismatch with the high-frequency precision required for matting. We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting. We show that strong image-conditioned matting capabilities can be acquired even from a pure text-to-image model without paired image-to-image pretraining. Our approach reuses the backbone's native patch embedding to jointly process the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte as spatially aligned tokens, enabling direct pixel-space prediction without additional condition encoders or adapters. With pixel-accurate synthetic supervision and real-image fine-tuning, PixelMatte predicts alpha mattes in a single forward pass. Two lightweight calibration modules, LayerShift and High-Pass Refiner, further improve regional opacity accuracy and boundary fidelity. Experiments across four benchmarks and two distinct pixel diffusion backbones demonstrate substantial improvements over existing methods, reducing SAD and gradient errors by 24-44% relative to RenderMatte on P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, while achieving competitive performance on AIM-500 and RenderMatte-2K. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of pixel-space generative priors for precision-sensitive image matting. Project page: https://white-yc.github.io/pixelmatte/.

</details>

## 8. Memory Is Not Ground Truth: Revising Navigation Authority under Unreliable VLM Evidence for Persistent Semantic Navigation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce a dependence-aware, reversible memory-update mechanism that avoids double-counting correlated misses and stops using unsupported memories as direct navigation goals without claiming that the object is absent. In three matched real-robot decision contexts, an episode-local baseline selected a stale memory as its navigation objective in every case, whereas our method selected semantic frontiers in all three.
- Authors: Sumukh Shrote, Shenbagaraj Kannapiran, Eric Eaton, Camillo Jose Taylor
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34915v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language models (VLMs) enable robots to reason about semantic and spatio-temporal context, verify task-relevant objects, and guide sensing and navigation. Persistent memory can reduce redundant search for field robots, but when should a memory continue to guide behavior? Repeated misses may suggest that a memory is stale. Our experiments show why this evidence can remain ambiguous under VLM-based perception. Using a Boston Dynamics Spot with four fixed cameras and a persistent spatio-temporal visual memory, we measure negative-verification behavior across targets, viewpoints, language queries, and repeated revisits. False-negative rates vary substantially across objects, nearby revisits often reproduce the same detector outcome, and some matched target-present and target-removed experiments yield identical miss histories. This ambiguity persists under changed observation geometry, identity-preserving query reformulation, and conditioning on a previously verified target image. These findings raise two questions: is the object still there, and is this memory still useful for navigation? We introduce a dependence-aware, reversible memory-update mechanism that avoids double-counting correlated misses and stops using unsupported memories as direct navigation goals without claiming that the object is absent. Later positive evidence can restore a memory's use for navigation. One resident onboard VLM is shared across memory verification, semantic frontier reasoning, and in-motion perception. In three matched real-robot decision contexts, an episode-local baseline selected a stale memory as its navigation objective in every case, whereas our method selected semantic frontiers in all three. A complementary trial re-verified the target during motion and promoted fresh memory. These results support revising memory actionability rather than interpreting failed detection as object absence.

</details>

## 9. Agentic LongLive: Asynchronous Agentic Memory for Long Video Generation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: On LongStory-Online, a new benchmark of 25 stories (857 shots) revealed one shot at a time, Agentic LongLive improves persistent-state accuracy of LongLive-2.0, its base generator, by 26.3 points, protagonist identity by 44.9, and revisited-place consistency by 40.4, at about 1% extra generation time on H100. The full system streams faster than real time on one 8xB200 node with the generator in BF16 (34.3 FPS), keeps image quality stable over 14-minute stories, and improves identity on two MiniMax-H3 generators, one of which, LongLive-H3, also runs in real time with it.
- Authors: Shaoteng Liu, Shuai Yang, Wei Huang, Luozhou Wang, Song Han, Yukang Chen
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34907v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Real-time autoregressive video generators stream minutes of video and accept new prompts on the fly, but struggle to keep people, objects, and places consistent over a long interactive story. Agent systems provide such memory for offline multi-shot generation, yet they read the whole script in advance, plan each shot before generating it, and regenerate failed shots, which a live stream does not allow. We present Agentic LongLive, an asynchronous agentic memory for long video generation. A memory agent on separate GPUs reads only the prompts revealed so far, never the generated video, and keeps a text state and evolving images for every person, object, and scene. From this entity memory it composes a global image and a per-shot memory image, which an auxiliary GPU prefills into the generator's attention sink before the next shot starts, and writes entity cards for the prompts that name them, so the video never waits for memory and video errors are not written back. A small language model, trained with reinforcement learning and an image reward calibrated using downstream video outcomes, decides what to remember before the next prompt is known. On LongStory-Online, a new benchmark of 25 stories (857 shots) revealed one shot at a time, Agentic LongLive improves persistent-state accuracy of LongLive-2.0, its base generator, by 26.3 points, protagonist identity by 44.9, and revisited-place consistency by 40.4, at about 1% extra generation time on H100. The full system streams faster than real time on one 8xB200 node with the generator in BF16 (34.3 FPS), keeps image quality stable over 14-minute stories, and improves identity on two MiniMax-H3 generators, one of which, LongLive-H3, also runs in real time with it. To our knowledge, it is the first real-time video generator with text and image memory kept by an LLM agent.

</details>

## 10. ProbFaS: Probabilistic Video Frame Selection for MLLMs via Multi-Fidelity Calibration

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Processing long videos with multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is prohibitively expensive, making informative frame selection essential for accurate video understanding. The default uniform sampling often misses task-relevant keyframes, while recent training-free selectors instead pick frames by how well each one matches the question under an image-text alignment (ITA) score.
- Authors: Tianru Zhang, Mayank Nautiyal, Hugo Eidmann, Prashant Singh, Ekta Vats
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34903v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Processing long videos with multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is prohibitively expensive, making informative frame selection essential for accurate video understanding. The default uniform sampling often misses task-relevant keyframes, while recent training-free selectors instead pick frames by how well each one matches the question under an image-text alignment (ITA) score. However, we show that ITA signals correspond poorly with the MLLM's true needs: their rankings correlate weakly with downstream accuracy and are even negatively correlated on over 40% of questions, whereas the MLLM's internal yes/no judgment yields a much stronger signal. We present ProbFaS, a training- and label-free probabilistic pipeline that fuses both signals for key frame selection. ProbFaS treats dense ITA scores as cheap, biased measurements and sparse MLLM judgments as accurate, costly ones, unifying them via co-kriging, a multi-fidelity Gaussian process, to calibrate relevance across all frames. A temporal determinantal point process then selects frames capturing relevance peaks while preserving temporal coverage. Across Video-MME, MLVU and LongVideoBench, ProbFaS is the most accurate among fourteen state-of-the-art training-free selectors and uniform sampling in 13 of 15 settings (8-128 frames), outperforming the strongest baseline by up to 3.1 percentage points. On MLVU and LongVideoBench, 32 ProbFaS frames match or surpass the top baseline at 128 frames and uniform sampling with up to 768 frames.

</details>
