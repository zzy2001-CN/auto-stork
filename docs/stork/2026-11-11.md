## 1. Where Do Vision-Language Models Get Left and Right? Position-Code Counterfactuals in Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL and InternVL3

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: A vision-language model may take left-right direction from the orientation of image content within patches, its vision transformer's (ViT) position code, or token positions and order in its language model (LLM). Ablations show the ViT code is necessary; we test sufficiency by mirroring only a position code.
- Authors: Enhao Cui, Junzhi Yang, Yaoshun Lin, Yuhan Feng, Zijian Chen
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14060v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

A vision-language model may take left-right direction from the orientation of image content within patches, its vision transformer's (ViT) position code, or token positions and order in its language model (LLM). Ablations show the ViT code is necessary; we test sufficiency by mirroring only a position code. In Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-4B and InternVL3-2B, mirroring ViT width positions reproduces the flipped-image answer on 84.3-93.2% of CV-Bench left-right questions (Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 87.5%, partly a left fallback). With two options, this also counts disruption; a half-grid shift flips most answers too. With the ViT code intact, LLM-side id and token-order mirrors change few left-right answers in all three series (a weak id test in Qwen2.5-VL). In Qwen2.5-VL-7B the effect sits in the global-attention ViT blocks, mostly the last. On What'sUp, flipping content and token order, not the position code, keeps more on/under than left-right answers at the flipped-image answer. Post hoc analyses qualify these results. On CV-Bench items with located objects, random permutations flip 18.9-31.0 points more answers when reversing the objects' order than when keeping it. On What'sUp, in Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL, the share of the left-right changes made by random permutations that land on the mirror relation is mostly below chance. Counted as returns to the original answer, the What'sUp contrast of support with left-right relations is clear only in InternVL3-2B. Most remaining errors give the same relation whichever object is asked about, suggesting that repair may target relation binding, object localization or use of the reference object.

## 2. HandsOnAid: Building and Evaluating an Offline First-Aid Assistant for Emergency Response Scenarios

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Fine-tuning improved the mean score by +1.08 over the base model to 2.34.
- Authors: Rezwan Islam Salvi, Saiyma Sittul Muna, Mushfiqur Rahman Mushfique
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14059v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Emergencies can occur where there is no phone signal and no ambulance nearby. A small language model on a phone can be helpful in such situations, but its advice needs to be safe and accurate. We built HandsOnAid, a 4-bit Gemma 2B model fine-tuned with QLoRA on FirstAidQA dataset, a corpus of 5,550 first-aid question-answer pairs. We compared seven configurations, including the base, 4-bit and 8-bit fine-tuned models, and versions with a length floor, safety gate, or retrieval. We used a 41-pair question-answer bank with medically reviewed references that followed ANZCOR guidelines, and a strict 0-5 score rubric, with twelve safety overrides. Four frontier LLM judges (GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, DeepSeek V4 Pro, GLM-5.3) blindly scored every answer, with 45 hidden controls used to check judge reliability. Fine-tuning improved the mean score by +1.08 over the base model to 2.34. The best configuration scored 2.51 out of 5 which was the fine-tuned 4-bit model with retrieval. A medical professional also conducted a blind evaluation of 40 answers across the base, the 4-bit fine-tuned, and the retrieval configurations, and the scores were similar to the LLM judges' average scores. A keyword audit found limited coverage of several details of ANZCOR guidelines in the training corpus, that are required by the rubric, which suggests that missing information in the training data could be a cause of the low scores. On a smartphone device (Pixel 7 Pro) with six CPU threads and no network connection, the 4-bit fine-tuned model produced its first displayable text in 6.86 seconds and a complete answer in 14.88 seconds on average over ten questions.

## 3. CURRENT: A Recurrent Neural Network for Concurrent Spatial and Temporal Super-Resolution of 4D Flow MRI

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Objective: To improve the spatial and temporal resolution of aortic 4D flow MRI through a unified deep learning framework for post-acquisition super-resolution (SR) of reconstructed velocity vector fields. Methods: We propose CURRENT, a recurrent neural network that performs spatial and temporal SR with a single network architecture.
- Authors: Luuk Jacobs, Pietro Dirix, Sebastian Kozerke
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14058v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Objective: To improve the spatial and temporal resolution of aortic 4D flow MRI through a unified deep learning framework for post-acquisition super-resolution (SR) of reconstructed velocity vector fields. Methods: We propose CURRENT, a recurrent neural network that performs spatial and temporal SR with a single network architecture. Supervised training was conducted using paired low- and high-resolution datasets generated by embedding computational fluid dynamics simulations of aortic flow into in vivo thoracic MRI backgrounds, followed by controlled spatial and temporal downsampling. CURRENT was evaluated for two-fold spatial and temporal SR against 4DFlowNet and for two-fold spatiotemporal SR against sequential spatial and temporal SR pipelines based on 4DFlowNet. Image-based and k-space-based quantitative metrics were considered and conventional interpolation were used as baselines. In addition, the impact of CURRENT's spatiotemporal SR on physics-informed relative pressure estimation was assessed. Finally, the ability of CURRENT to generalize from synthetic training data to in vivo acquisitions was investigated. Results: CURRENT achieved superior spatial SR performance and comparable temporal SR performance to dedicated 4DFlowNet models while providing a unified solution for both tasks. Simultaneous spatiotemporal SR using CURRENT yielded more accurate relative pressure estimates than either spatial or temporal SR alone and outperformed sequential 4DFlowNet-based spatial and temporal SR approaches. Furthermore, the proposed model demonstrated promising generalization to in vivo 4D flow MRI data despite being trained exclusively on synthetic datasets. Conclusion: CURRENT provides a unified framework for spatial and temporal SR of 4D flow MRI. The proposed approach has the potential to enhance physics-based analysis and broaden the applicability of accelerated 4D flow MRI.

## 5. From Show Programs to Linked Data

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: This paper presents a workflow for transforming such documents into structured, interoperable data by combining vision-language models (VLMs), a custom extension of the Linked Art ontology for the performing arts, and different approaches for automatic semantic data annotation.
- Authors: Clarisse Bardiot, Bernard Jacquemin, Jacob Hart, George Bruseker, Antonios Lagarias, Pierre-Carl Langlais, Brandon Farnsworth, Jeanne Fras
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14037v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Many heritage institutions hold extensive collections of show programs. Too numerous to be cataloged individually, they remain difficult to access and are still largely undigitized. This paper presents a workflow for transforming such documents into structured, interoperable data by combining vision-language models (VLMs), a custom extension of the Linked Art ontology for the performing arts, and different approaches for automatic semantic data annotation. Using the Festival d'Avignon programs (1947--present), preserved at the Biblioth{è}que nationale de France, as a case study, we demonstrate how VLMs achieve more than 98% text transcription accuracy from heterogeneous heritage document images and how constraining large language model generation through a knowledge graph significantly reduces hallucinations in semantic annotation. We argue that this workflow opens new possibilities for large-scale, interoperable performing arts historiography, including the creation of catalogues raisonn{é}s for performing artists, a form of scholarship common in visual arts but absent in performing arts studies.

</details>

## 6. ScaleBrain: A Scale-Consistent Framework for EEG Foundation Models

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Recent EEG foundation models have shown that multi-scale representations derived from fixed-length patches can improve EEG representation learning. Existing frameworks commonly construct temporal scales through feature-level operations.
- Authors: Minyu Zhou, Yajing Wu, Yongqiang Tang, Wang Jia, Wensheng Zhang
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14035v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Foundation models pre-trained on large unlabeled electroencephalography (EEG) corpora have emerged as a transformative paradigm for Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) applications. Recent EEG foundation models have shown that multi-scale representations derived from fixed-length patches can improve EEG representation learning. Existing frameworks commonly construct temporal scales through feature-level operations. However, these operations leave the correspondence between learned features and scale-specific characteristics of the original EEG signals implicit. Moreover, when the self-supervised objective is not aligned with the multi-scale input representations, information at some temporal scales may receive insufficient supervision during pre-training. In light of this, we propose ScaleBrain, a scale-consistent pre-training framework that explicitly coordinates multi-scale representations of EEG from both the model input and optimization objective sides. Specifically, ScaleBrain introduces multi-scale EEG tokenization to represent the same EEG segment through analysis windows with different temporal spans and shared temporal anchors, and pairs these representations with scale-specific reconstruction to align with the multi-scale input structure and provide supervision across temporal resolutions during self-supervised learning. We further develop an axial spatio-temporal attention mechanism to contextualize the fused multi-scale representation along spatial and temporal dimensions. Extensive evaluations on six public BCI benchmarks against 12 representative baselines show that ScaleBrain achieves the highest mean Balanced Accuracy on five datasets and ranks third on the remaining one, with only 5.0M parameters, demonstrating strong performance and favorable parameter efficiency.

</details>

## 7. An Alignment Microscope for Imaging Rotational Wavepacket Dynamics

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce a new method for encoding polarization-angle-dependent molecular alignment signals directly into the transverse spatial profile of a femtosecond laser beam. The approach employs a pump-probe setup in a common-path geometry.
- Authors: J. Strohaber
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14034v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce a new method for encoding polarization-angle-dependent molecular alignment signals directly into the transverse spatial profile of a femtosecond laser beam. The approach employs a pump-probe setup in a common-path geometry. A radial-azimuthal polarization converter placed in the probe beam produces a doughnut-shaped beam whose local polarization varies continuously with azimuthal angle, thereby encoding the full range of linear polarization orientations from 0 to $2π$ within a single transverse beam profile. Consequently, the angular dependence of the molecular response can be recorded simultaneously rather than reconstructed through sequential measurements at different polarization angles. By varying only the pump-probe delay, this spatial encoding enables single-shot acquisition of the complete angular response at each delay and real-time visualization of the evolving molecular alignment pattern as the delay stage is scanned. This technique has enabled the experimental realization of an alignment microscope.

## 8. HiPFuse: Real-Time LiDAR Semantic Segmentation for Robotic Perception on Edge Devices

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We present HiPFuse, a point-BEV fusion framework that exchanges point and height-partitioned BEV features across horizontal scales, combining point-wise detail with 2D spatial context from one LiDAR scan.
- Authors: Haiyang Wu, George Vosselman, Ville Lehtola
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14025v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Real-time robotic perception requires accurate LiDAR semantics under limited resources. Bird's-eye-view (BEV) aggregation is efficient but can mix differently labeled surfaces in columns. We present HiPFuse, a point-BEV fusion framework that exchanges point and height-partitioned BEV features across horizontal scales, combining point-wise detail with 2D spatial context from one LiDAR scan. Fixed height assignments preserve separate vertical aggregation groups, with auxiliary supervision of initial BEV features using targets from the same 3D cells. A dual-scale BEV context module adds broader context from a coarser grid. On SemanticKITTI and MCD, HiPFuse achieves 63.7% and 62.5% mIoU at 14.2 and 22.4 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin, respectively, exceeding the 10 Hz scan rate. Separate training and evaluation show indoor applicability. Replacing only the baseline semantic front end with HiPFuse in fixed robotic systems reduces odometry absolute trajectory error RMSE by 10.6% on average across two outdoor sequences and increases accepted-loop recall by 8.7 percentage points on SemanticKITTI. Qualitative semantic maps show more continuous road labeling. Our code is available at https://github.com/haiyang2022/HiPFuse.

</details>

- Authors: Xue Tang, Xin Wu, Yalan Zhang, Guodong Shi
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14024v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

In this paper, we study Goncharov-type Rota-Baxter operators in the framework of group-graded Hopf pi-algebras. By constructing convolution pi-algebras as the core calculus tool, we systematically derive fundamental algebraic properties of Hopf pi-algebras, including antipode anti-homomorphism and generalized homomorphism compatibility. On this basis, we establish the axiomatic system of two classes of graded Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 and weight -1, prove their bijective correspondence via the antipode, construct several non-trivial examples, and present two derived structures: descendant Hopf pi-algebras and image Hopf pi-subalgebras. This work enriches the operator theory of graded Hopf algebras and provides algebraic constructions for related research on graded Yang-Baxter equations.

## 10. A Stage-Wise Explainable Pipeline for Arteriolar-to-Venular Ratio Estimation and Retinopathy Screening

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: The pipeline provides a transparent framework for investigating retinal vascular biomarkers, with further validation needed on larger, clinically graded cohorts.
- Authors: Sharan Sharmeela Karuppannan, Yoga Sri Varshan, Subin Sahayam, Umarani Jayaraman
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14013v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

The arteriolar-to-venular diameter ratio (AVR) is a clinically motivated marker of retinal vascular health. We present a five-stage pipeline for AVR estimation and exploratory retinopathy screening from fundus images, with inspectable intermediate outputs at each stage. The pipeline combines vessel and artery-vein masks, optic-disc localization, vessel-calibre estimation using a minimal-chord boundary walk, and AVR computation from dominant arterial and venular branches. An explicit threshold-based rule produces the screening decision, enabling errors to be traced to individual processing stages. On an exploratory cohort of 30 HRF images, we report 96.7% accuracy, an F1-score of 0.968 and ROC-AUC of 0.978. Ablation studies and an exploratory comparison with VascX examine the effects of vessel pairing, calibre measurement and aggregation. Interpretation is limited by the small cohort, proxy disease labels and absence of independent reference calibre measurements. The pipeline provides a transparent framework for investigating retinal vascular biomarkers, with further validation needed on larger, clinically graded cohorts.

</details>
