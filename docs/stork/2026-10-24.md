<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Controllable 3D part generation is pivotal for digital asset creation, yet existing methods lack the flexibility to accommodate diverse user workflows requiring varying levels of control. In this paper, we introduce FlexPart, a unified image-to-3D part generation framework that supports variable-granularity geometric conditions within a single model. By seamlessly integrating heterogeneous inputs, ranging from sparse points to dense bounding boxes and masks, via gated adaptive modulation, FlexPart unlocks a powerful cross-modal synergy. We also propose an asymmetric geometric guidance mechanism that leverages strong spatial priors to align representations across granularities, improving geometric consistency and facilitating inter-task synergy. Under mask conditioning on PartVerseXL, FlexPart reduces Part CD by 16.6% relative to TripoSG + SAM3D and by 10.5% relative to OmniPart, while maintaining competitive whole-mesh quality. The code can be found at https://github.com/Gucci233/FlexPart

## 2. Odd radio circle J1555+2726: Observational evidence for shock re-acceleration of fossil plasma

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Odd radio circles (ORCs) are peculiar extragalactic sources recently discovered. They are characterised by one or more bright rings extending on scales of hundreds of kiloparsecs, and are filled with fainter emission.
- Authors: L. Bruno, B. Koribalski, R. P. Norris, A. Ivleva, T. Venturi, A. Botteon, C. J. Riseley, K. Dolag
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31664v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Context. Odd radio circles (ORCs) are peculiar extragalactic sources recently discovered. They are characterised by one or more bright rings extending on scales of hundreds of kiloparsecs, and are filled with fainter emission. A massive elliptical galaxy lies at the centre of ORCs. While their formation mechanism is debated, a shock is plausibly involved. Aims. We aim to constrain the nature of ORC J1555+2726 (ORC4) through the analysis of its morphological and spectral properties. Methods. We utilised Low Frequency ARray (LOFAR), upgraded Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (uGMRT), and MeerKAT radio data at 50-1284 MHz to obtain images and spectral index maps at high resolution and sensitivity. Results. Both the resolution and observing frequency affect the observed morphology. In particular, the brightness contrast between the ring and the filling emission becomes progressively smoother from higher to lower frequencies. We measured an integrated spectrum of slope $α=1.06\pm 0.03$, a radio power of $P_{\rm 150}= (5.4\pm 0.5)\times 10^{25} \; {\rm W \; Hz^{-1}}$ at 150 MHz, and a radial spectral flattening down to $α\sim0.5$ at the ring location. However, we inferred a negative (convex) spectral curvature and a peculiar spectral shape in radio colour-colour diagrams, which imply spectral flattening towards high frequencies. These properties cannot be explained by shock acceleration of thermal particles and standard ageing. Conclusions. The observed features suggest that a mixture of electrons with different spectra coexist within ORC4 volume, likely associated with shock re-acceleration of a fossil relativistic reservoir. The ring emission is plausibly dominated by higher-energy electrons re-accelerated at the shock front, while lower-energy re-accelerated electrons are more uniformly distributed across the whole source (possibly ellipsoidal) volume.

</details>

## 3. SpectralCanary: Embarrassingly Simple Low-Frequency Traces for T2I Dataset-Use Auditing

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Existing approaches often trade off verifiability, fidelity, robustness, and deployability. We introduce $\textbf{SpectralCanary}$, which uses a smooth low-pass mask to blend a fixed canary image into every collection image while leaving captions unchanged and requiring neither auxiliary generation nor per-image optimization.
- Authors: Sy-Tuyen Ho, Anirudh Satheesh, Soham Chawathe, Huy Nghiem, Yongkyun Lee, Andrew Adams Schoen, Furong Huang
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31659v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Auditing whether a text-to-image (T2I) model was fine-tuned on a particular image collection is difficult: a model can absorb collection-level visual characteristics without reproducing training images. Proactive dataset-use auditing plants a shared signal in a dataset before release, then later checks whether that signal appears in a suspect model's generated outputs. Existing approaches often trade off verifiability, fidelity, robustness, and deployability. We introduce $\textbf{SpectralCanary}$, which uses a smooth low-pass mask to blend a fixed canary image into every collection image while leaving captions unchanged and requiring neither auxiliary generation nor per-image optimization. A lightweight verifier detects the transferred signal in suspect-model outputs. Across eight tests on the Pokemon dataset and three realistic data transformations, spanning two architectures families, SpectralCanary achieves the highest average per-generation TPR@$1\%$ FPR ($91.1\%$) and fixed threshold model-level accuracy using 30 generations ($94.8\%$) among the evaluated methods. Across five datasets spanning artistic, medical, satellite, and fashion domains with SD3.5, SpectralCanary averages $92.4\%$ per-generation TPR@$1\%$ FPR while preserving the released images (SSIM $0.989$, LPIPS $0.022$) with a near-zero mean change FID (mean $Δ$FID $0.05$) across datasets. Matched-energy ablations on Pokemon and ROCOv2 support our design choice, low-frequency placement transfers more reliably compared to mid/high-frequency placement. Under the evaluated adaptive attacks, the most effective removal requires aggressive modification and incurs more than $25$ $Δ$FID points. These results identify low-frequency visual signals as effective carriers for collection-level T2I audit traces.

- Authors: Ava Pun, Kevin Dai, Sally Chen, Maxwell Jones, Ruixuan Liu, Kangle Deng, Changliu Liu, Maneesh Agrawala
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31651v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Frontier coding agents are becoming increasingly adept at 3D modeling. Yet, they still face challenges when building objects meant to be assembled in the physical world, often creating invalid assemblies with floating, colliding, or disconnected parts. We argue that providing agents with tools for assembling builds and verifying their physical feasibility is sufficient for them to produce physically valid assemblies that still adhere to input prompts. Focusing on the domain of LEGO brick builds, we present BrickWright, a framework for generating physically feasible brick builds from text or image prompts using pre-trained agents, where a build is physically feasible if it uses only real parts, has no colliding or floating parts, and forms a connected structure if possible. BrickWright guides agents to create builds by writing code with BrickLayer, a Python toolkit with two main components: an ergonomic brick assembly API that halves inference cost compared to working in raw LDraw, and a validator that provides feedback on physical feasibility. Whereas baseline agents achieve physical validity rates ranging from only 9% (GPT-6 Luna) to 69% (GPT-6 Astra), BrickWright-guided agents achieve 100% validity on a sample of 512 prompts, while improving prompt adherence for lightweight agents and maintaining it for stronger agents. Additionally, they outperform specialized models on the text-to-brick task. Code is available on our project page: https://avalovelace1.github.io/brickwright/

## 5. Gate3DGen: A Pre-processing Model that Selectively Purifies Real-World Images for 3D Generation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce Gate3DGen, a selective pre-processing model that gates image inputs to existing 3D generators without retraining them. On the synthetic benchmark, the purifier improves image fidelity and downstream 3D geometry in only 2.3s on average, reducing TRELLIS.2 mesh distance by 61.5% relative to degraded inputs.
- Authors: Xiaolin Li, Zihao Huang, Fanpeng Meng, Chen Yang, Guanjun Wu, Kai Zhang, Jiemin Fang, Qi Tian
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31649v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Single-image 3D generation has achieved high fidelity on curated benchmarks, yet degrades sharply on real-world images with uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions. Purifying such inputs requires deciding when to intervene and preserving the visual cues needed for 3D generation, while leaving clean inputs unchanged. We introduce Gate3DGen, a selective pre-processing model that gates image inputs to existing 3D generators without retraining them. A degradation-aware router bypasses inputs classified as clean and extracts features to guide purification of the remaining inputs. Conditioned on these features, a 3D-oriented one-step purifier purifies degraded inputs with spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity, contours, and textural cues. To provide paired supervision, we construct a synthetic data engine that renders clean views of 3D assets and synthesises composite degradations. On the synthetic benchmark, the purifier improves image fidelity and downstream 3D geometry in only 2.3s on average, reducing TRELLIS.2 mesh distance by 61.5% relative to degraded inputs. VLM evaluation and human preferences on real photographs further support identity preservation and degradation correction. More demos and code are available at https://supra-3d.github.io/gate3dgen.

## 6. OmniRay: Camera-Agnostic Monocular 3D Reconstruction with Composite Ray Fields

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Monocular models recover 3D geometry from a single image in one pass, but most assume a pinhole camera and produce distorted geometry on the wide-angle imagery produced by fisheye and panoramic lenses. Camera-aware models remove this assumption by predicting a per-pixel ray field.
- Authors: Namitha Guruprasad, Abhay Yadav, Cheng Peng, Rama Chellappa, Anand Bhattad
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31645v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Monocular models recover 3D geometry from a single image in one pass, but most assume a pinhole camera and produce distorted geometry on the wide-angle imagery produced by fisheye and panoramic lenses. Camera-aware models remove this assumption by predicting a per-pixel ray field. However, they represent different camera geometries using a single fixed basis to capture both the overall camera projection and its finer variations. We argue that camera geometry can be viewed as having a hierarchy of complexity for representing rays. We present OmniRay, a feed-forward model whose Ray Module is composed of three components: a monotone radial base that captures radially symmetric structure, a spherical-harmonic residual for smooth deviations from it, and a free-form residual for remaining local variation. These components are composed as successive rotations on the sphere, allowing the representation to accommodate diverse camera geometries. Given a single image, OmniRay predicts a ray direction and an up-to-scale radial distance for every pixel, from which a 3D point cloud is recovered without needing camera information at inference time. Extensive experiments across different camera configurations demonstrate that OmniRay establishes a new state-of-the-art in camera-agnostic monocular 3D reconstruction.

</details>

## 8. Nonvanishing of 2-primary Kolyvagin classes via simultaneous quadratic twists

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Let $E/\mathbf{Q}$ be an elliptic curve, let $K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ satisfy the Heegner hypothesis with $D<-4$, and fix a modular parametrization $\varphi$ with $\varphi(\infty)=O$. Under surjectivity modulo $2^L$, with $L\ge 2$, and a cohomological exclusion condition at level $4$, we construct a finite set of primes $T$ such that, writing $d_T=\prod_{q\in T}(-q)$ and $n_T=\prod_{q\in T}q$, the $2$-Selmer dimensions of $E^{d_T}$ and $E^{d_TD}$ are $0$ and $1$.
- Authors: Zhiqi Sun
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31631v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Let $E/\mathbf{Q}$ be an elliptic curve, let $K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ satisfy the Heegner hypothesis with $D<-4$, and fix a modular parametrization $\varphi$ with $\varphi(\infty)=O$. Under surjectivity modulo $2^L$, with $L\ge 2$, and a cohomological exclusion condition at level $4$, we construct a finite set of primes $T$ such that, writing $d_T=\prod_{q\in T}(-q)$ and $n_T=\prod_{q\in T}q$, the $2$-Selmer dimensions of $E^{d_T}$ and $E^{d_TD}$ are $0$ and $1$. The support size is bounded in terms of the initial Selmer dimensions, independently of $L$. Inspired by the work of Tian and Tian--Yuan--Zhang on congruent numbers, we construct a point $Q_T$ on the terminal twist and identify its Kummer class with the standard mod-$2$ Kolyvagin class. Using the low-corank $2$-converse and the $2$-primary Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer formula, we prove that its index $m_T$ in the full Mordell--Weil lattice satisfies $v_2(m_T)=v_2(c_\varphi)+\mathfrak{i}(E,K)$. The optimal Manin constant theorem supplies a $2$-indivisible parametrization $\varphi_0$, for which $κ^{\mathrm{std}}_{n_T,1}(\varphi_0)\ne 0$ if and only if $\mathfrak{i}(E,K)=0$. When the intrinsic index vanishes, the minimum terminal support size is $R=\min\{d_2(E),d_2(E^D)\}$, and the normalized standard classes have order $2^M$ for $1\le M\le L$. Full $2$-adic Galois image then gives strong nonvanishing.

## 9. Ray-by-Ray: Fine-Grained Resource Management for GPU Ray Tracing Units

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: However, ray tracing introduces substantial control and data divergence. To address inefficiency caused by mismatches between SIMT and RT granularities, we propose Ray-by-Ray, which decomposes on-chip buffers into the granularity of individual rays and introduces lightweight management structures that enable more warps to run in parallel without increasing buffer capacity.
- Authors: Junrui Pan, Cesar Avalos Baddouh, Kai Ze Ee, Timothy G. Rogers
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31630v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Ray tracing (RT) generates photorealistic images by simulating the physical behavior of light as it interacts with surfaces in a virtual scene. In film production and high-end rendering pipelines, this technique computes global illumination, reflections, refractions, and soft shadows by tracing millions of light rays per frame and accurately modeling their bounces and attenuation. Contemporary GPUs incorporate specialized ray tracing hardware to accelerate key operations in the traversal pipeline. By accelerating these stages in hardware, the unit enables real-time ray tracing for complex scenes that previously required offline computation. However, ray tracing introduces substantial control and data divergence. Each ray may traverse a different set of nodes in the acceleration structure, encounter distinct materials, or terminate at varying depths. This irregularity reduces thread coherence and exposes a fundamental disparity between the GPU SIMT and per-thread execution model of dedicated RT units, where each ray progresses independently. In this work, we seek to validate assumptions made in open-source infrastructure and identify potential inefficiencies in resource management within the ray tracing pipeline. To address inefficiency caused by mismatches between SIMT and RT granularities, we propose Ray-by-Ray, which decomposes on-chip buffers into the granularity of individual rays and introduces lightweight management structures that enable more warps to run in parallel without increasing buffer capacity. This approach improves ray tracing unit utilization and increases memory-level parallelism. Experimental results show that Ray-by-Ray achieves an average performance improvement of 1.91x over the baseline, with an area overhead of 0.19\%.

</details>

## 10. F2G-Pose: Geometry-Aware Foundation Feature Lifting for Direct RGB-D Category-Level Object Pose Estimation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Current solutions struggle to balance reliability and efficiency: methods based on intermediate structures are prone to error propagation, while diffusion models suffer from high inference latency and complex post-hoc candidate extraction. To address this, we present F2G-Pose, a real-time direct, single-pass framework that predicts category-level pose, metric size, and camera-frame completed shape from a single RGB-D observation in one feed-forward pass.
- Authors: Jinyu Zhang, Haitao Lin, Jiashu Hou, Xiangyang Xue, Yanwei Fu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31623v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

This paper studies category-level RGB-D object pose estimation, which recovers an object's 3D rotation and translation without instance-specific CAD models or reference views. This task is highly ambiguous due to intra-class variations, symmetries, and partial observations. Current solutions struggle to balance reliability and efficiency: methods based on intermediate structures are prone to error propagation, while diffusion models suffer from high inference latency and complex post-hoc candidate extraction. To address this, we present F2G-Pose, a real-time direct, single-pass framework that predicts category-level pose, metric size, and camera-frame completed shape from a single RGB-D observation in one feed-forward pass. F2G-Pose lifts dense visual foundation features to partial point clouds, encodes local geometry with DGCNN tokenization, and integrates object-level structure with a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer. Joint size prediction and camera-frame shape completion encourage metric consistency through dense geometric supervision. With its task-specific network trained only on pose-annotated synthetic SOPE data and its frozen RADIO backbone externally pretrained, F2G-Pose achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SOPE and ROPE while running at 28 FPS for pose-only inference, and further demonstrates strong camera-frame geometry on HouseCat6D and real-object transfer on HANDAL. Analyses of latency, robustness, shape completion, and GenPose++ candidate behavior further demonstrate the efficiency and reliability of F2G-Pose, with real-world manipulation experiments demonstrating deployment potential under segmented RGB-D inputs. The project page, code, and pretrained models are made available at https://jrryzh.github.io/F2G-Pose/.

</details>
