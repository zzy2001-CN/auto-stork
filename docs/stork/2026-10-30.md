## 1. CoraLive: Ultra-Low-Power Underwater Vision for Long-term Coral Reef Monitoring

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: To achieve this, CoraLive introduces a dual-rate sensing scheme combining frequent low-resolution frames with sparse high-fidelity samples and a content-adaptive update mechanism that extracts and stores only compact, task-specific data, eliminating the need to save raw video frames.
- Authors: Sitong Shen, Chenshu Wu
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39472v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Effective coral reef protection and restoration demands continuous, long-term underwater monitoring. However, current solutions largely rely on fragmented, manual labor and costly infrastructure, or are severely limited by constrained battery lifespans. In this paper, we present CoraLive, an ultra-low-power underwater camera system for long-term, continuous coral reef monitoring. CoraLive presents a decoupled underwater-shore co-design that minimizes on-board computation and storage, offloading heavy reconstruction and segmentation tasks to offline shore-side processing. To achieve this, CoraLive introduces a dual-rate sensing scheme combining frequent low-resolution frames with sparse high-fidelity samples and a content-adaptive update mechanism that extracts and stores only compact, task-specific data, eliminating the need to save raw video frames. We implement CoraLive on a custom STM32U575-based dual-camera prototype with device-level computation and storage optimization. Compared with raw recording, CoraLive reduces daily storage by $4.51\times$ and average power by $7.78\times$, extending battery lifetime from 20.4 to 159.5~days while retaining high-accuracy segmentation and reconstruction. Further validated in a real-world underwater deployment, we envision CoraLive as a pioneering step for ubiquitous long-term coral reef underwater monitoring.

## 2. Calibrating Attention-Based Token Pruning for Efficient Multi-Image Synthesis in UMMs

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication

To address these issues, we propose CATP, the first training-free context-compression method tailored to UMM generation, which \textbf{C}alibrates the discrepancies of general \textbf{A}ttention-based \textbf{T}oken \textbf{P}runing for better quality-efficiency trade-off.
- Authors: Zimeng Wu, Qijian Tian, Penghui Du, Changqian Yu, Jiaxin Chen, Yunhong Wang
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39467v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) enable powerful multi-image synthesis but incur substantial computation from long visual contexts. While token pruning offers a direct route to accelerating inference, existing methods are primarily adapted from understanding tasks, suffering from insufficient reference image exploration, confused conditional guidance, and pooling-induced ambiguity that degrades generation quality. To address these issues, we propose CATP, the first training-free context-compression method tailored to UMM generation, which \textbf{C}alibrates the discrepancies of general \textbf{A}ttention-based \textbf{T}oken \textbf{P}runing for better quality-efficiency trade-off. Concretely, building on independent layer-wise pruning during denoising, we introduce Guidance-Consistent Pruning (GCP) to synchronize pruning decisions across conditioning branches, and a Taylor-Calibrated Attention Metric (TCAM) that derives a theoretically grounded second-order correction for pooling-induced bias. In addition, a Fast Scoring Operator (FSO) fuses the calibration into tiled online Softmax to reduce pruning cost. Extensive experiments across representative UMMs and benchmarks demonstrate that CATP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art token-reduction methods, achieving up to 2.09$\times$ attention-core speedup.

## 3. Integrating Heterogeneous Echocardiography AI Outputs in Language Space: Learnable Late Fusion with a Supervised LLM

- Reason: recent publication

- Authors: Ryo Takizawa, Tempei Kabayama, Chiemi Yamazaki, Shun Kitamura, Ryo Matsuoka, Yuta Ando, Satoshi Kodera, Norihiko Takeda
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39464v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

View- and target-specific echocardiography AI models capture useful but complementary information and may not achieve sufficient predictive performance in isolation. A learned ensemble is a natural way to combine these outputs, but heterogeneous models typically require each output to be converted into a binary decision, probability, confidence score, or fixed-length feature vector. We developed a supervised large language model (LLM)-based late-fusion module that directly aggregates view-specific predicted finding statements and predicted measurements in language space and learns a case-level fusion function. In patient-level three-fold cross-validation of 859 examinations from 646 patients, the proposed method integrated heterogeneous outputs within a single framework, performed comparably to the best-performing reference strategy evaluated, and outperformed a general-purpose LLM without task-specific training. These results suggest that a supervised LLM can combine multiple upstream models without model-specific output conversion or feature engineering that requires medical knowledge.

## 4. Smooth Action Chunking Via Movement-Primitive Representations for Imitation Learning

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: Imitation learning has become a widely adopted approach for vision-based robot policies, owing to its conceptual simplicity, scalability, and ability to leverage large-scale datasets.
- Authors: Shota Nomura, Takayuki Osa, Yasuhiro Kato, Kazuki Ota, Naoki Shitanda, Tatsuya Harada
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39451v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Imitation learning has become a widely adopted approach for vision-based robot policies, owing to its conceptual simplicity, scalability, and ability to leverage large-scale datasets. As these policies have grown more capable, action chunking has become their standard interface. At each decision step the policy outputs several future actions at once. Backbones from diffusion policies to large vision-language-action models all share it. Whatever backbone produces the chunk, its temporal structure is learned only implicitly. The commanded trajectory is therefore jerky both within a chunk and between successive chunks. On real hardware this appears as visible vibration and as repeated saturation of the follower's velocity limit. We address this issue by incorporating a movement-primitive-based trajectory representation into action chunking. Specifically, a trajectory segment is represented as a linear combination of smooth basis functions, yielding smooth trajectories by construction while leaving the policy architecture, training objective, and inference procedure unchanged. The same representation also yields a closed-form, inference-time correction for the second source of jerk. We condition the predicted coefficients so that the new chunk passes through a via-point at the previous chunk's last commanded action. This correction needs no retraining and, unlike asynchronous Real-Time Chunking, applies to policies queried in one shot. We instantiate it on a one-shot (ACT) and an iterative (DiT) backbone and evaluate in simulation and on a real bimanual ALOHA platform. The representation lowers within-chunk jerk by an order of magnitude on the one-shot backbone and by 2.4 times on the iterative one, with task success statistically non-inferior. The conditioning then removes 73-84% of the boundary discontinuity, at negligible per-step cost.

</details>

- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39441v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Medical AI learns from the answers doctors record, but rarely from how they interpret the evidence to reach them. Here we introduce PathCowork, a framework that turns pathologists' narrated examinations into supervision for visual grounding and procedures that guide diagnostic AI agents. Synchronized gaze, narration and navigation link visual findings to the process of interpreting them. Across 48 sessions from 16 pathologists, the platform captured 1,028 minutes of slide review. Using these data, we trained PathCowork-1T for server deployment and PathCowork-27B for local deployment, with improved visual grounding. Across five organ-specific evaluations, agents combining this grounding with recorded diagnostic procedures achieved primary task scores averaging 17.9 and 24.1 percentage points higher than Claude Code and Codex, respectively, with shorter processing times and lower inference costs. Compared to the state-of-the-art multiple-instance learning AIs, the PathCowork agent-proposed regions achieved higher prostate-grading scores with limited slide labels. PathCowork can also help construct pathology AI benchmarks where questions derived from the recordings identified difficulties in recognizing viewing context and rejecting inapplicable premises. In summary, PathCowork proposes a new paradigm to scale up the data collection and train pathology AI through transparent reasoning from diagnostic sessions, opening a path towards systems that learn directly from routine clinical practice.

## 6. Timbre: Reliability-aware harmonic consensus for label-free multimodal test-time rexinforcement learning

- Authors: Haotian Lyu, Haoyu Wang, Ru Wang, Feng Ji, Ziwen Li, Jiaxian Guo, Mingming Gong
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39439v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, weakly supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Label-free test-time reinforcement learning adapts vision--language models using rewards derived from their own predictions. However, misinterpreted visual evidence and sensitivity to textual formulation can produce a consistent but incorrect consensus that majority voting reinforces. Cross-view aggregation can expose such errors, but image and text transformations may themselves degrade task-relevant evidence to different extents. We introduce \method, a reliability-aware harmonic consensus framework for multimodal test-time reinforcement learning. \method constructs a $2\times2$ grid combining original and perturbed images with direct-solving and reframing modes, selects pseudo-labels through weighted harmonic aggregation, and adapts modality-level weights using batch-averaged, temporally smoothed shifts in pseudo-label confidence as a heuristic reliability signal. We formulate consensus as a weighted $χ^2$-barycenter problem whose unique minimizer is the normalized weighted harmonic aggregate, without requiring correct-answer invariance. An exact support--dispersion decomposition characterizes when harmonic aggregation preserves or corrects arithmetic rankings, while a sensitivity analysis establishes how modality reweighting changes pairwise ranking margins, informing interpretation of the heuristic updates. Across six benchmarks and three Qwen3-VL model scales, \method outperforms TTRV in 16 of 18 benchmark--model settings on adaptation instances and 17 of 18 settings on held-out instances under matched estimation and optimization rollout counts. These results demonstrate improved target-task adaptation and transfer to unseen instances without further updates.

## 7. FDSML: Formula-Driven Supervised Metric Learning with Parameter-Aware Triplet Loss

- Authors: Hina Otake, Shinei Arakawa, Keitaro Tanaka, Yoshihiro Fukuhara, Hirokatsu Kataoka, Shigeo Morishima
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39423v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, scribble supervised segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Formula-Driven Supervised Learning (FDSL) enables vision pre-training without real images, but existing methods cast pre-training as classification on discretized labels, discarding continuity in the generative process. We propose Formula-Driven Supervised Metric Learning (FDSML), which derives relational supervision from image-generation parameters beyond discrete class labels. FDSML combines FineFractalDB, our refined fractal dataset with higher intra-class variation and more stable structures, with a Parameter-Aware Triplet (PA-Triplet) Loss that assigns positives and negatives according to distances in the underlying Iterated Function System parameter space. This objective trains embeddings to respect parameter-derived relative similarity orderings. Experiments show that FDSML improves transferability to image retrieval while remaining competitive on downstream classification and improving robustness under several corruption settings. On ResNet-50, Recall@1 improves from 65.6% to 74.0% on Stanford Online Products and from 56.2% to 78.4% on Stanford Cars over FDSL pre-training. These results suggest that parameter-derived relational supervision provides a stronger synthetic pre-training signal than label-based classification for transfer beyond recognition.

- Authors: Uihyeon Maeng, Jonghun Kim, Hyunjin Park
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39414v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Accurate lung tumor segmentation in computed tomography (CT) is essential for treatment planning, response assessment, and quantitative imaging analysis. However, 3D lung tumor segmentation remains challenging because tumors occupy only a small portion of the whole volume and exhibit heterogeneous appearances across anatomical locations. In patch-based training and sliding-window inference for 3D medical image segmentation, location-dependent heterogeneity is further amplified because cropped image patches provide limited global anatomical context, making it difficult for a model to adapt its feature processing according to the anatomical region from which each patch is extracted. To address this limitation, we propose an Anatomical Prior-guided Routing Module (APRM) for lung tumor segmentation. For each image patch, we obtain anatomical prior information of regional occupancy and deterministically select an anatomical routing gate. The selected gate then performs sparse feature routing over a shared expert adapter pool, enabling anatomy-specific feature adaptation while sharing expert capacity across regions. We evaluate APRM on two datasets with various backbone networks and demonstrate consistent improvements over baseline models. Furthermore, feature visualizations show that APRM produces different feature responses depending on patch location, indicating that the proposed routing mechanism effectively adapts feature processing according to anatomical context.

## 9. SymKL: A Unified Representation-Space Measure of Signal Degradation

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: We introduce SymKL, a unified full-reference degradation measure based on implicit neural representations (INRs). The proposed formulation requires only the reference and degraded observation, without knowledge of the corruption process or degradation severity, making it applicable across images, audio, and 3D data.
- Authors: Dhananjaya Jayasundara, Sudarshan Rajagopalan, Vishal M. Patel
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39403v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Quantifying degradation between a pristine signal and its degraded observation is fundamental to signal processing, yet existing full-reference measures are inherently modality-specific. We introduce SymKL, a unified full-reference degradation measure based on implicit neural representations (INRs). An INR is fitted once to the pristine reference and characterized by its diagonal Fisher information, which captures the local information geometry of the learned representation. Evaluating the Fisher information with the pristine reference and a degraded observation yields two parameter-sensitivity profiles whose symmetric KL divergence defines the degradation score. The proposed formulation requires only the reference and degraded observation, without knowledge of the corruption process or degradation severity, making it applicable across images, audio, and 3D data. We further derive an exact closed-form expression for the diagonal Fisher of SIREN, enabling efficient computation. Experiments on image, speech, and volumetric 3D benchmarks demonstrate that SymKL accurately tracks degradation severity, remains monotonic where conventional metrics saturate or fail, and provides a unified modality-agnostic measure of signal degradation. Code will be made publicly available after the review process.

## 10. CASE: Concept-Aware Segmentation from Positive and Negative Visual Exemplars

- Source: arXiv
- Score: 20.0
- Reason: recent publication
- Innovation: To study SVPS, we introduce CASE (Concept-Aware Segmentation from Positive and Negative Visual Exemplars), a benchmark built on COCO and PACO. To address this, we present Concept Prompt Adaptation (CPA), a conservative LoRA-based adaptation baseline that improves positive-negative prompt interpretation on CASE while remaining competitive under positive-only few-shot segmentation.
- Authors: Yi-Jen Tsai, Yen-Yu Lin, Chien-Yao Wang
- Year: 2026
- URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39396v1
- Matched queries: semi-supervised medical image segmentation, pseudo-label medical image segmentation

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Few-shot semantic segmentation typically identifies target objects based on positive support examples of a single semantic class. In practice, however, positive cues alone are often underspecified: for instance, "red cars" may be confused with cars of all colors, "vehicles" may include cars that should be excluded, and "material-defined objects" may span multiple categories. Fulfilling precise requests requires positive exemplars to provide inclusion evidence and negative exemplars to act as intent-defining constraints for exclusion and disambiguation. We formulate this setting as Specified Visual Prompt Segmentation (SVPS), where the target concept is specified by both positive and negative visual prompts. In this work, visual exemplars refer to support-image regions specified by bounding-box prompts. To study SVPS, we introduce CASE (Concept-Aware Segmentation from Positive and Negative Visual Exemplars), a benchmark built on COCO and PACO. CASE encompasses six category- and attribute-level task families: exclusion, isolation, union, attribute exclusion, attribute isolation, and attribute union. We evaluate SAM 3 under this setting and find that, despite its powerful positive-only few-shot segmentation capability, frozen SAM 3 does not consistently treat negative visual prompts as exclusion evidence. To address this, we present Concept Prompt Adaptation (CPA), a conservative LoRA-based adaptation baseline that improves positive-negative prompt interpretation on CASE while remaining competitive under positive-only few-shot segmentation. Code is available at https://github.com/WongKinYiu/FSS-CASE.

</details>
